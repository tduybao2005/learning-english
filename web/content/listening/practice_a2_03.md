F: Good evening, everyone, and thank you for coming to this short talk on saving electricity at home. My name is Ryan, and I work as an energy adviser for the local council. Second, try switching to LED light bulbs, since they use far less electricity than older bulbs and last much longer. Third, unplug chargers and small appliances when they're not in use, because they still use a little electricity even on standby. Fourth, only boil the amount of water you actually need in the kettle, rather than filling it every time. Fifth, wash clothes at thirty degrees instead of a higher temperature whenever possible, as this uses noticeably less energy. Sixth, try to run the washing machine and dishwasher with a full load, rather than running them half empty. Seventh, in winter, close your curtains at night to keep the heat inside your rooms for longer. Eighth, check that your fridge door seals properly, because a fridge that lets cold air escape has to work much harder. Ninth, consider drying clothes on a rack instead of using a tumble dryer, since dryers use a lot of electricity. And tenth, if you're leaving the flat for more than a day, turn off appliances at the wall rather than leaving them on standby. If you follow even half of these tips, you could save a noticeable amount on your monthly bill. The council also offers a free home energy check, where an adviser visits your home and gives more personal advice. You can book that service by calling the number on our website. Another simple habit is to defrost your freezer regularly, since a build-up of ice makes it use more electricity to stay cold. It also helps to use a microwave instead of an oven for small meals, since microwaves heat food much more quickly and use less power overall. Finally, setting your thermostat just one degree lower can make a real difference over a whole winter without you even noticing the change. Thank you again for listening, and I hope these tips help you and your flatmates save some money this year.
