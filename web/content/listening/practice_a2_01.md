F: Hello everyone, and welcome to your first swimming lesson. My name is Mr. Davies, and I'll be your coach for the next eight weeks. Before we get in the water, I want to give you a few important tips. First, always warm up for a few minutes before swimming — simple stretches for your arms and legs are enough. Second, breathing is the most important skill for a beginner, so we will practise breathing exercises at the side of the pool before we practise strokes. Third, please don't hold your breath while swimming — it actually makes you more tired, not less. When you learn front crawl, remember to turn your head to the side to breathe, rather than lifting your whole head up, because lifting your head makes your legs sink. For your legwork, try to kick from the hip, not just from the knee, and keep your legs fairly straight but relaxed. If you swallow water and start to panic, simply stop, stand up if you can, or hold onto the poolside and take a few slow breaths. We will also practise floating on your back today, because floating helps you relax and trust the water. Please remember to shower before entering the pool — this is a health rule at all leisure centres, not just here. It's also a good idea to remove any jewellery before you get in, since rings and necklaces can be lost in the water or even cause a small injury. If you wear glasses, we do have a small box of swimming goggles you can borrow for today, so don't worry if you forgot yours. After the lesson, please dry your hair properly before going outside, especially in the colder months, so you don't catch a cold. We will finish every lesson with five minutes of gentle floating and relaxing, which helps your body recover. Finally, always listen for the lifeguard's whistle: one long whistle means stop and listen, and three short whistles mean an emergency, so leave the pool immediately. I'll see you all in the water in five minutes — please get changed now.
